# Performance Principles (generic)

Generic, industry-common framing for individual-contributor performance —
used to help phrase Check-in evidence in terms of outcomes. This is general
guidance, **not** any employer's confidential rubric.

> If your employer publishes an internal rubric, keep your own copy **local
> and gitignored** (e.g. `references/rubric.confidential.md`) — do not commit
> it here.

## Core idea: impact over effort
Performance is judged by outcomes that move team or company priorities
forward — not by activity or hours. "I did a lot of X" is weaker than
"X produced Y." Lead every point with the outcome.

## A common three-axis lens
Many IC frameworks look at three dimensions. Map evidence to whichever fits
naturally; don't force it:

- **Strategy** — prioritizing the highest-impact work, making sensible
  tradeoffs, de-risking (e.g. feature-flagging a risky rollout), surfacing
  risks early.
- **Execution** — delivering with speed *and* quality, on time, communicating
  progress/risks clearly, seeing work through end-to-end.
- **Leadership** — influence beyond your own tasks: being a reliable go-to,
  sharing knowledge, unblocking others, driving cross-team coordination.

## Framing tips
- For a delivered feature: name the customer need, the outcome, and adoption.
- For quality/security: scope (how many, which branches) and current status.
- For behind/in-progress goals: state the blocker plainly — surfacing risk
  clearly is itself good execution, not something to hide.
