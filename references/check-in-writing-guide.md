# How to Write a Strong Check-in

A generic, best-practice guide for writing a periodic performance check-in
(self-reflection). No employer-specific content — just principles that travel.
Pair it with `performance-principles.md` (framing) and `content-preferences.md`
(rules).

---

## The typical questions

Most check-ins ask three things:
1. **Results** — what you delivered toward your goals, with metrics/outcomes; and
   if you're behind on a goal, what's getting in the way.
2. **Growth** — how you're developing and working differently; new skills; how
   you're using new tools (e.g. AI) to improve.
3. **Manager feedback** — what's working and what would help you do better work.

Plus a **Goals** section (goal / how success is measured / impact).

---

## Question 1 — Results

- **Lead every result with the clearest sign of progress** — a metric, an
  outcome, or a milestone — not the activity that produced it.
  - Weak: "I worked on the checkout flow and fixed many bugs."
  - Strong: "Cut checkout errors from 4% to 0.6%, which lifted completed orders
    ~3%."
- **Organize by goal.** One short block per goal.
- **Own it end-to-end.** Note where you drove something across its full scope,
  not just one slice.
- **Behind on a goal? Say so plainly, with the reason and your plan.** Surfacing
  a risk clearly is good execution, not a weakness.
  - "The redesign slipped ~3 weeks when a dependency landed late; I re-sequenced
    the work, locked a firm date, and cut scope to the two highest-value pieces."
- **Keep the receipts out of the prose.** Ticket/PR IDs and counts belong in the
  evidence attachment, not the narrative.

## Question 2 — Growth

- **Show how your scope or skills widened**, with a concrete example.
- **Tie growth to your role's competency framework** where it fits — e.g.
  prioritization/tradeoffs (**Strategy**), speed + quality + clear communication
  (**Execution**), influence/knowledge-sharing (**Leadership**).
- **Be concrete about new tools/skills** (e.g. "I use AI to draft test scaffolds
  and review my own diffs before raising a PR, which frees time for the harder
  design work").
- **Name one honest development area** you're actively working on.

## Question 3 — Manager feedback

- **Use Situation → Behavior → Impact (SBI):** the situation, the observable
  behavior, and its effect — honest but respectful, with a real example.
- **Balance it:** one thing that's working + one specific, low-risk ask (clearer
  priorities, more specific feedback, guidance as you take on more).
- **Keep asks level-appropriate.** Early-career: avoid asks that read as reaching
  (lead/mentor) or as wanting off the project (more cross-team). Frame growth as
  deepening skills within the current team.

---

## Goals

For each goal, state: the **goal**, **how success is measured** (specific,
honest metrics), and the **impact** on customers/team/business. Keep metrics
defensible ("no known critical regressions" beats an unprovable absolute).

---

## Tone & honesty rules

- **Impact over activity.** Outcomes move the needle; effort alone doesn't.
- **No vanity metrics** in the prose (no "I merged N PRs", no "only 1 revert").
- **Never invent numbers.** If a metric isn't real, omit it or flag the gap.
- **Honesty on status.** In-progress is in-progress; don't claim unshipped as
  delivered.
- **Soft, professional, concise.** No inflated words.

## Evidence (the proof sheet)

Keep a one-page attachment: tables of the tickets/PRs behind each claim, with
status and links. It makes everything traceable and keeps the narrative clean.

## Refine after drafting

Re-read as a skeptical manager: does each point lead with impact? Is anything
overstated or unverifiable? Any vanity metrics? Right altitude? Fix wording; be
honest about gaps that are real work, not phrasing.

## Prepare for the conversation

Pick the 2-3 points to lead with; anticipate likely questions ("what was
hardest?", "where are you behind?", "what's next?", "what do you need from
me?"); prepare 2-3 questions to ask your manager.

## Peer feedback (optional, high-value)

Ask a few people you worked with for input — on something you delivered, a
growth area, or a working relationship. Ask **one focused question**, request a
**specific example**, frame it as **SBI**, and give a short deadline. Make it
easy to say yes.
